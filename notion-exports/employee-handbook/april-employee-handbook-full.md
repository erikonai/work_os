# I. General Policies
## 1. Employment Relationship
Employment at the Company is "AT-WILL," which means that any employee may voluntarily resign at any time and that the Company may terminate an employee at any time, with or without cause or notice. This Handbook and any other documents of the Company do not constitute contracts of employment, either expressed or implied.
## 2. Open Door Policy
The Company is committed to maintaining a professional and supportive work environment. To that end, we encourage an open-door approach in which employees may raise questions, concerns, suggestions, or complaints and expect a timely, thoughtful, and respectful response from management.
Employees are expected to treat one another with professionalism and mutual respect and are encouraged to provide constructive feedback in an appropriate manner. If an employee has a concern regarding workplace conditions, a decision affecting their employment, or the application of Company policies or practices, they are encouraged to raise the issue promptly. In most cases, employees should begin by discussing the matter with their immediate manager. However, if the employee is not comfortable doing so, or if the concern is not resolved, the employee may raise the issue with Human Resources or another member of management, as appropriate.
The Company takes employee concerns seriously and will review them as appropriate under the circumstances. To the fullest extent permitted by law, the Company prohibits retaliation against any employee who, in good faith, raises a concern or participates in a review of such concern.
## 3. Equal Employment Opportunity
It is the policy of the Company to ensure that all persons receive equal employment opportunities solely on the basis of their skills, abilities, job-related qualifications, competence and job performance.
The Company is committed to ensuring that all employees and applicants for employment are afforded equal employment opportunities without regard to race (including, but not limited to, traits historically associated with race, such as hair texture and protected hair styles, etc.); age; religion (including, but not limited to, religious attire and facial hair); creed; sex/gender (including, but not limited to, pregnancy and breastfeeding); sexual preference or orientation, gender identity or expression (including transgender status); height; weight; military/uniformed service status; immigration status; citizenship status; disability status; predisposing genetic characteristics; familial status; ancestry; marital status; partnership status; caregiver status; status as a victim of domestic violence/sexual violence/stalking; sexual and reproductive health decisions, consumer credit history; salary history; unemployment status; arrest or conviction record (in accordance with applicable law); on the basis of an individual's known relationship or association with members of a protected class; on the basis of off-duty and legal use of consumable products, "political activities," and "recreational activities" in accordance with applicable law; or any other basis prohibited by applicable federal, state and/or local law.
It is the obligation of every employee to adhere to the spirit as well as the letter of these practices. The Company will ensure that all terms and conditions of employment (including, but not limited to, recruitment, advertising, hiring, layoffs, termination, placements, compensation, promotion, demotion, recall, training, leaves of absence and Company sponsored educational, social and recreational programs and events) are made and administered in a nondiscriminatory manner.
Employees with questions or concerns about equal employment opportunities in the workplace are encouraged to bring these issues to the attention of Human Resources or a member of the senior leadership team. Any conduct of any employee, including a supervisory employee, in violation of this policy will result in appropriate disciplinary action, up to and including an unpaid suspension or termination of employment. No retaliation will be taken against an employee who makes a good faith report of illegal discrimination or other conduct that violates this policy. Conversely, any person who knowingly makes a false claim of discrimination shall also be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including an unpaid suspension or termination of employment.
## 4. Reasonable Accommodations
The Company complies with the Americans with Disabilities Act, the Pregnant Workers Fairness Act, and any other applicable federal, state, and local laws providing for nondiscrimination in employment against qualified individuals with disabilities. The Company will work with employees with disabilities or other needs to develop reasonable accommodations that allow an individual the opportunity to perform the essential functions of their job in a safe and efficient manner, unless the accommodation would impose an undue hardship. The Company will consider requests for reasonable accommodation on a case-by-case basis and will engage in a timely and good faith interactive process to determine if it can make a reasonable accommodation without undue hardship.
Employees are encouraged to inform Human Resources of any mental or physical disability, pregnancy, childbirth and related conditions, or if you are a victim of domestic violence or sex crimes and need to seek shelter or guidance. The Company encourages individuals to come forward and request reasonable accommodations. If an employee thinks they will need a reasonable accommodation in order to perform essential job functions, they should inform Human Resources as soon as possible. This procedure is voluntary and confidential. All information that you provide to the Company about your need for an accommodation will be held in confidence and will not be disclosed to anyone without your permission, to the extent permissible by law. The Company may request verification of information from your physician relating to your need for an accommodation.
An employee who has questions about this policy or believes that they have been discriminated against based on disability should notify Human Resources. All such inquiries or complaints will also be treated as confidential to the extent permissible by law.

Nothing in this policy is intended to limit, reduce, or otherwise diminish any rights available to employees under federal, state, or local law.

The Company will provide reasonable accommodations for an employee's sincerely held religious beliefs, practices, or observances that conflict with a work requirement, unless doing so would impose an undue hardship on the operation of april's business. ### Sexual Harassment
Sexual harassment is a form of gender-based discrimination that is unlawful under federal, state, and (where applicable) local law. Sexual harassment includes harassment on the basis of sex, sexual orientation, self-identified or perceived sex, gender expression, gender identity, and the status of being transgender. Sexual harassment is not limited to sexual contact, touching, or expressions of a sexually suggestive nature. Sexual harassment includes all forms of gender discrimination including gender role stereotyping and treating employees differently because of their gender.
Understanding gender diversity is essential to recognizing sexual harassment because discrimination based on sex stereotypes, gender expression and perceived identity are all forms of sexual harassment. The gender spectrum is nuanced, but the three most common ways people identify are cisgender, transgender, and non-binary. A cisgender person is someone whose gender aligns with the sex they were assigned at birth. Generally, this gender will align with the binary of male or female. A transgender person is someone whose gender is different than the sex they were assigned at birth. A non-binary person does not identify exclusively as a man or a woman. They might identify as both, somewhere in between, or completely outside the gender binary. Some may identify as transgender, but not all do. Respecting an individual's gender identity is a necessary first step in establishing a safe workplace.
Sexual harassment is generally unlawful when it subjects an individual to inferior terms, conditions, or privileges of employment. Harassment does not need to be severe or pervasive to be illegal. It can be any harassing behavior that rises above petty slights or trivial inconveniences. Every instance of harassment is unique to those experiencing it, and there is no single boundary between petty slights and harassing behavior. However, the Human Rights Law specifies that whether harassing conduct is considered petty or trivial is to be viewed from the standpoint of a reasonable victim of discrimination with the same protected characteristics.
While the standard for sexual harassment may vary from state to state, note that any behavior in which an employee or covered individual is treated worse because of their gender (perceived or actual), sexual orientation, or gender expression is considered a violation of april's policy. The intent of the behavior, for example, making a joke, does not neutralize a harassment claim. Not intending to harass is not a defense. The impact of the behavior on a person is what counts. Sexual harassment includes any unwelcome conduct which is either directed at an individual because of that individual's gender identity or expression (perceived or actual), or is of a sexual nature when:
- The purpose or effect of this behavior unreasonably interferes with an individual's work performance or creates an intimidating, hostile or offensive work environment. The impacted person does not need to be the intended target of the sexual harassment;
- Employment depends implicitly or explicitly on accepting such unwelcome behavior; or
- Decisions regarding an individual's employment are based on an individual's acceptance to or rejection of such behavior. Such decisions can include what shifts and how many hours an employee might work, project assignments, as well as salary and promotion decisions.
There are two main types of sexual harassment:
- Behaviors that contribute to a **hostile work environment** include, but are not limited to, words, signs, jokes, pranks, intimidation, or physical violence which are of a sexual nature, or which are directed at an individual because of that individual's sex, gender identity, or gender expression. Sexual harassment also consists of any unwanted verbal or physical advances, sexually explicit derogatory, or discriminatory statements which an employee finds offensive or objectionable, causes an employee discomfort or humiliation, or interferes with the employee's job performance.
- Sexual harassment also occurs when a person in authority tries to trade job benefits for sexual favors. This can include hiring, promotion, continued employment or any other terms, conditions, or privileges of employment. This is also called **quid pro quo** harassment.
Any employee or covered individual who feels harassed is encouraged to report the behavior so that any violation of this policy can be corrected promptly. Any harassing conduct, even a single incident, can be discrimination and is covered by this policy.
### Examples of Sexual Harassment
The following describes some of the types of acts that may be unlawful sexual harassment and that are strictly prohibited. **This list is just a sample of behaviors and should not be considered exhaustive.** Any employee who believes they have experienced sexual harassment, even if it does not appear on this list, should feel encouraged to report it:
**A. Physical Harassment**
1. Touching, pinching, patting, kissing, hugging, grabbing, brushing against another employee's body, or poking another employee's body; or
2. Rape, sexual battery, molestation, or attempts to commit these assaults, which may be considered criminal conduct outside the scope of this policy (please contact local law enforcement if you wish to pursue criminal charges).
**B. Sexual Advances, Propositions, or Other More Subtle Behaviors**
1. Unwanted sexual comments, advances, or propositions, such as:
  1. Requests for sexual favors accompanied by implied or overt threats concerning the target's job performance evaluation, a promotion, or other job benefits;
    1. This can include sexual advances/pressure placed on a service industry employee by customers or clients, especially those industries where hospitality and tips are essential to the customer/employee relationship;
  2. Subtle or obvious pressure for unwelcome sexual activities; or
  3. Repeated requests for dates or romantic gestures, including gift-giving.
2. Sexually oriented gestures, noises, remarks or jokes, or questions and comments about a person's sexuality, sexual experience, or romantic history which create a hostile work environment. This is not limited to interactions in person. Remarks made over virtual platforms and in messaging apps when employees are working remotely can create a similarly hostile work environment.
3. Sex stereotyping, which occurs when someone's conduct or personality traits are judged based on other people's ideas or perceptions about how individuals of a particular sex should act or look:
  1. Remarks regarding an employee's gender expression, such as wearing a garment typically associated with a different gender identity; or
  2. Asking employees to take on traditionally gendered roles, such as asking a woman to serve meeting refreshments when it is not part of, or appropriate to, her job duties.
**C. Discriminatory Comments, Displays or Publications in the Workplace and Hostile Actions**
1. Verbal or written communications that could be interpreted as slurs, slang words, threats or insults directed toward a particular individual or group of individuals, or inquiries into an individual's sexuality or sexual experiences, or commentaries about a person's body.
2. Sexual or discriminatory displays or publications anywhere in the workplace, such as:
  1. Displaying pictures, posters, calendars, graffiti, objects, promotional material, reading materials, or other materials that are sexually demeaning or pornographic.
    1. This includes such sexual displays on workplace computers or cell phones and sharing such displays while in the workplace and also extends to the virtual or remote workspace and can include having such materials visible in the background of one's home during a virtual meeting.
3. Hostile actions taken against an individual because of that individual's sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, or gender expression, such as:
  1. Interfering with, destroying, or damaging a person's workstation, tools or equipment, or otherwise interfering with the individual's ability to perform the job;
  2. Sabotaging an individual's work;
  3. Bullying, yelling, or name-calling;
  4. Intentional misuse of an individual's preferred pronouns; or
  5. Creating different expectations for individuals based on their perceived identities. For example: dress codes that place more emphasis on women's attire; leaving parents/caregivers out of meetings.
### Targets of Sexual Harassment
Sexual harassment can occur between any individuals, regardless of their sex or gender. Harassment does not have to be between members of the opposite sex or gender. Employees and all covered individuals described earlier in the policy are protected under this policy. **Harassers can be anyone in the workplace.** A manager, a direct report, or a coworker can all be harassers. Anyone else in the workplace can also be harassers including an independent contractor, contract worker, vendor, client, customer, patient, constituent, or visitor.
Sexual harassment does not happen in a vacuum and discrimination experienced by an employee can be impacted by biases and identities beyond an individual's gender. For example:
- Placing different demands or expectations on black women employees than white women employees can be both racial and gender discrimination;
- An individual's immigration status may lead to perceptions of vulnerability and increased concerns around illegal retaliation for reporting sexual harassment; or
- Past experiences as a survivor of domestic or sexual violence may lead an individual to feel re-traumatized by someone's behaviors in the workplace.
Individuals bring personal history with them to the workplace that might impact how they interact with certain behavior. It is especially important for all employees to be aware of how words or actions might impact someone with a different experience than their own in the interest of creating a safe and equitable workplace.
### Where Can Sexual Harassment Occur?
Unlawful sexual harassment is not limited to the physical workplace itself. It can occur while employees are traveling for business or at employer or industry sponsored events or parties. Calls, texts, emails, and social media usage by employees or covered individuals can constitute unlawful workplace harassment, even if they occur away from the workplace premises, on personal devices, or during non-work hours.
Sexual harassment can occur when employees are working remotely from home as well. Any behaviors outlined above that leave an employee feeling uncomfortable, humiliated, or unable to meet their job requirements may constitute harassment even if the employee or covered individual is at home when the harassment occurs. Harassment can happen on virtual meeting platforms, in messaging apps, and after working hours between personal cell phones.
### No Retaliation
Retaliation is unlawful and is any action by an employer or supervisor that punishes an individual upon learning of a harassment claim, that seeks to discourage a worker or covered individual from making a formal complaint or supporting a sexual harassment or discrimination claim, or that punishes those who have come forward. These actions need not be job-related or occur in the workplace to constitute unlawful retaliation. For example, threats of physical violence outside of work hours or disparaging someone on social media would be covered as retaliation under this policy. Other examples of retaliation may include, but are not limited to:
- Demotion, termination, denying accommodations, reduced hours, or the assignment of less desirable shifts;
- Publicly releasing personnel files;
- Refusing to provide a reference or providing an unwarranted negative reference;
- Labeling an employee as "difficult" and excluding them from projects to avoid "drama";
- Undermining an individual's immigration status; or
- Reducing work responsibilities, passing over for a promotion, or moving an individual's desk to a less desirable office location.
Such retaliation is unlawful under federal, state, and (where applicable) local law. Federal, state, and (where applicable) local law protects any individual who has engaged in "protected activity." Protected activity occurs when a person has:
- Made a complaint of sexual harassment or discrimination, either internally or with any government agency;
- Testified or assisted in a proceeding involving sexual harassment or discrimination under the Human Rights Law or any other anti-discrimination law;
- Opposed sexual harassment or discrimination by making a verbal or informal complaint to management, or by simply informing a supervisor or manager of suspected harassment;
- Reported that another employee has been sexually harassed or discriminated against; or
- Encouraged a fellow employee to report harassment.
Even if the alleged harassment does not turn out to rise to the level of a violation of law, the individual is protected from retaliation if the person had a good faith belief that the practices were unlawful. No individual will suffer any reprisals or retaliation from the Company for reporting or making any complaints in good faith regarding any incidents of alleged, actual, or perceived harassment or discrimination under this policy, or for participating in any investigation of incidents of discrimination or harassment.
However, the retaliation provision is not intended to protect persons making intentionally false charges of harassment. Consistent with april's ethical standards, individuals who are dishonest in an investigation will be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including an unpaid suspension or termination of employment.
### Reporting Sexual Harassment
Any employee or covered individual is encouraged to report harassing or discriminatory behavior to a manager, any member of management, or Human Resources. Anyone who witnesses or becomes aware of potential instances of sexual harassment should report such behavior to a supervisor, any member of management, or Human Resources.
Reports of sexual harassment may be made verbally or in writing. A written complaint may be found at Section V ("Complaint Form for Reporting Sexual Harassment") of this Handbook if an employee would like to use it, but the complaint form is not required. Employees who are reporting sexual harassment on behalf of other employees may use the complaint form and should note that it is on another employee's behalf. A verbal or otherwise written complaint (such as an email) on behalf of oneself or another employee is also acceptable.
Employees and covered individuals who believe they have been a target of sexual harassment may at any time seek assistance in additional available forums, as explained below in the section on Legal Protections.
### Supervisors and Managers Have Special Responsibilities
Supervisors and managers have a responsibility to prevent sexual harassment and discrimination. **All supervisors and managers who receive a complaint or information about suspected sexual harassment, observe what may be sexually harassing or discriminatory behavior, or for any reason suspect that sexual harassment or discrimination is occurring, are required to report such suspected sexual harassment to Human Resources.** Managers and supervisors should not be passive and wait for an employee to make a claim of harassment. If they observe such behavior, they must act.
Supervisors and managers will be disciplined if they engage in sexually harassing or discriminatory behavior themselves. Supervisors and managers will also be disciplined for failing to report suspected sexual harassment or allowing sexual harassment to continue after they know about it. Supervisors and managers will also be subject to discipline for engaging in any retaliation.
While supervisors and managers have a responsibility to report harassment and discrimination, supervisors and managers must be mindful of the impact that harassment and a subsequent investigation has on victims. Being identified as a possible victim of harassment and questioned about harassment and discrimination can be intimidating, uncomfortable and re-traumatizing for individuals. Supervisors and managers must accommodate the needs of individuals who have experienced harassment to ensure the workplace is safe, supportive, and free from retaliation for them during and after any investigation.
### Bystander Intervention
Any employee witnessing harassment as a bystander is encouraged to report it. **A supervisor or manager who is a bystander to harassment is required to report it.** There are five standard methods of bystander intervention that can be used when anyone witnesses harassment or discrimination and wants to help:
- A bystander can interrupt the harassment by engaging with the individual being harassed and distracting them from the harassing behavior;
- A bystander who feels unsafe interrupting on their own can ask a third party to help intervene in the harassment;
- A bystander can record or take notes on the harassment incident to benefit a future investigation;
- A bystander might check in with the person who has been harassed after the incident, see how they are feeling and let them know the behavior was not ok; and
- If a bystander feels safe, they can confront the harassers and name the behavior as inappropriate. When confronting harassment, physically assaulting an individual is never an appropriate response.
Though not exhaustive, and dependent on the circumstances, the guidelines above can serve as a brief guide of how to react when witnessing harassment in the workplace. Any employee witnessing harassment as a bystander is encouraged to report it. A supervisor or manager that is a bystander to harassment is required to report it.
### Complaint Procedure
The Company encourages reporting of all perceived incidents of harassment, whether the person reporting is the victim or an observer, and regardless of the offender's identity or position. The Company provides its employees with a convenient and reliable method for reporting incidents of discrimination or harassment, including sexual harassment. If employees feel that they have been or are being harassed or discriminated against, they should immediately report the complained-of conduct to their manager, any member of management, or Human Resources. Employees may report in verbal or written form, and may use the Complaint Form at Section V of this Handbook.
There is no need to follow any formal chain of command when reporting a complaint. In addition, employees are encouraged (but not required) to inform the alleged harasser that the behavior is unwelcome. In many instances, the person is unaware that their conduct is offensive and when so advised can easily and willingly correct the conduct so that it does not recur. Should the alleged harassment occur at a time other than during normal business hours, the complaint should be voiced as early as practicable, preferably on the first business day following the alleged incident.
Any employee who witnesses or becomes aware of such objectionable conduct by any other employee (including, without limitation, supervisors and managers), vendor, or client is required to immediately advise their supervisor to ensure that such conduct does not continue. Any supervisor who becomes aware of possible sexual or other improper harassment must immediately notify Human Resources. In addition to being subject to discipline if they engaged in sexually harassing conduct themselves, supervisors and managers will be subject to discipline and liability for failing to report suspected sexual harassment or otherwise knowingly allowing sexual harassment to continue.
Employees who make complaints in bad faith may be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.
### Investigative Procedure
**Investigative Process:** All complaints or information about sexual harassment will be investigated, whether that information was reported in verbal or written form. Any reported allegation of harassment will be investigated promptly. The investigation may include individual interviews with the parties involved and, where necessary, with individuals who may have observed the alleged conduct or may have relevant knowledge. The investigation may also include a review of all relevant documents, including electronic communications. All relevant parties will be given the opportunity to express their understanding of the facts. All individuals involved, including those making a harassment claim, witnesses, and alleged harassers deserve a fair and impartial investigation. Therefore, the Company expects the full cooperation of all employees in connection with any investigation concerning a complaint made under this policy. The complaint and investigation will be handled with sensitivity, and confidentiality will be maintained throughout the investigative process to the extent practical and appropriate under the circumstances. However, the Company may need to disclose pertinent information to certain parties, including the alleged harasser.
While the process may vary from case to case, investigations will be done in accordance with the following steps. Upon receipt of a complaint, Human Resources, or a designated representative of the Company:
1. Will conduct a prompt review of the allegations, assess the appropriate scope of the investigation, and take any interim actions (for example, instructing the individual(s) about whom the complaint was made to refrain from communications with the individual(s) who reported the harassment), as appropriate. If complaint is verbal, request that the individual completes the complaint form in writing. If the person reporting prefers not to fill out the form, the Company will prepare a complaint form or equivalent documentation based on the verbal reporting;
2. Will take steps to obtain, review, and preserve documents sufficient to assess the allegations, including documents, emails or phone records that may be relevant to the investigation. The Company will consider and implement appropriate document request, review, and preservation measures, including for electronic communications;
3. Will seek to interview all parties involved, including any relevant witnesses;
4. Will create written documentation of the investigation (such as a letter, memo or email), which contains the following:
  1. A list of all documents reviewed, along with a detailed summary of relevant documents;
  2. A list of names of those interviewed, along with a detailed summary of their statements;
  3. A timeline of events;
  4. A summary of any prior relevant incidents disclosed in the investigation, reported or unreported; and
  5. The basis for the decision and final resolution of the complaint, together with any corrective action(s).
5. Will keep the written documentation and associated documents in a secure and confidential location;
6. Will promptly notify the individual(s) who reported the harassment and the individual(s) about whom the complaint was made that the investigation has been completed and implement any corrective actions identified in the written document; and
7. Will inform the individual(s) who reported of the right to file a complaint or charge externally as outlined in the next section.
**Responsive Action:** Upon the conclusion of the investigation, the Company will promptly notify the individual who complained of its determination and implement corrective action. Misconduct constituting sexual harassment will be dealt with appropriately. Responsive action may include, for example, training, referral to counseling, and disciplinary action such as warnings, reprimands, withholding of a promotion or pay increase, reassignment, temporary suspension without pay, compensation adjustments, or termination, as the Company may believe appropriate under the circumstances. Written records will be maintained in confidence to the extent practical and appropriate. The keeper of the records may vary depending on who filed the complaint. Written records will normally be maintained for at least the same period as other personnel records are maintained.
**Appeals Process:** If a party to a complaint does not agree with its resolution, that party should file a written complaint, addressed to the attention of Human Resources setting forth their reasons for the appeal.
**False or Malicious Accusations:** False and malicious complaints of harassment (as opposed to complaints which, even if erroneous, are made in good faith), will not be tolerated and may be the subject of appropriate disciplinary action.
### Legal Protections
Sexual harassment is not only prohibited by the Company, but it is also prohibited by state, federal, and (where applicable) local law. Aside from the internal process at the Company, covered individuals may also choose to pursue legal remedies with the following governmental entities.
**Local Police Department**
If the harassment involves unwanted physical touching, coerced physical confinement, or coerced sex acts, the conduct may constitute a crime. Those wishing to pursue criminal charges are encouraged to contact their local police department. You may reach the local police department by dialing 911.
**United States Equal Employment Opportunity Commission**
The United States Equal Employment Opportunity Commission (EEOC) enforces federal anti-discrimination laws, including Title VII of the 1964 federal Civil Rights Act, 42 U.S.C. § 2000e et seq. An individual can file a complaint with the EEOC anytime within 300 days from the most recent incident of harassment. There is no cost to file a complaint with the EEOC. The EEOC will investigate the complaint and determine whether there is reasonable cause to believe that discrimination has occurred. If the EEOC determines that the law may have been violated, the EEOC will try to reach a voluntary settlement with the employer. If the EEOC cannot reach a settlement, the EEOC (or the Department of Justice in certain cases) will decide whether to file a lawsuit. The EEOC will issue a Notice of Right to Sue permitting workers to file a lawsuit in federal court if the EEOC closes the charge, is unable to determine if federal employment discrimination laws may have been violated, or believes that unlawful discrimination occurred but does not file a lawsuit.
Individuals may obtain relief in mediation, settlement or conciliation. In addition, federal courts may award remedies if discrimination is found to have occurred. In general, private employers must have at least 15 employees to come within the jurisdiction of the EEOC.
An employee alleging discrimination at work can file a "Charge of Discrimination." The EEOC has district, area, and field offices where complaints can be filed. Contact the EEOC by calling 1-800-669-4000 (TTY: 1-800-669-6820), visiting their website at [www.eeoc.gov](http://www.eeoc.gov) or via email at [info@eeoc.gov](mailto:info@eeoc.gov).
If an individual filed an administrative complaint with the NYSDHR, the NYSDHR will automatically file the complaint with the EEOC to preserve the right to proceed in federal court.
**In New York: New York State Division of Human Rights (the "NYSDHR")**
The New York State Human Rights Law ("HRL"), N.Y. Executive Law, art. 15, § 290 et seq., applies to all employers in New York State and protects employees and covered individuals, regardless of immigration status. A complaint alleging violation of the Human Rights Law may be filed either with the NYSDHR or in New York State Supreme Court. Complaints of sexual harassment filed with the NYSDHR may be submitted any time within three (3) years of the harassment. If an individual does not file a complaint with NYSDHR, they can bring a lawsuit directly in state court under the Human Rights Law, within three (3) years of the alleged sexual harassment. An individual may not file with NYSDHR if they have already filed a HRL complaint in state court. Complaining internally to The Company does not extend your time to file with NYSDHR or in court. The three years are counted from the date of the most recent incident of harassment. You do not need an attorney to file a complaint with the NYSDHR, and there is no cost to file with NYSDHR.
NYSDHR will investigate your complaint and determine whether there is probable cause to believe that sexual harassment has occurred. Probable cause cases receive a public hearing before an administrative law judge. If sexual harassment is found after a hearing, NYSDHR has the power to award relief. Relief varies but it may include requiring your employer to take action to stop the harassment, or repair the damage caused by the harassment, including paying of monetary damages, punitive damages, attorney's fees, and civil fines. NYSDHR's main office contact information is: NYS Division of Human Rights, One Fordham Plaza, Fourth Floor, Bronx, New York 10458. You may call (718) 741-8400 or visit: [www.dhr.ny.gov](http://www.dhr.ny.gov).
Go to [dhr.ny.gov/complaint](http://dhr.ny.gov/complaint) for more information about filing a complaint with the NYSDHR. The website has a digital complaint process that can be completed on your computer or mobile device from start to finish. The website has a complaint form that can be downloaded, filled out, and mailed to the NYSDHR as well as a form that can be submitted online. The website also contains contact information for DHR's regional offices across New York State.
Call the NYSDHR sexual harassment hotline at 1(800) HARASS3 for more information about filing a sexual harassment complaint. This hotline can also provide you with a referral to a volunteer attorney experienced in sexual harassment matters who can provide you with limited free assistance and counsel over the phone.

## 9. Personal Relationships at Work
It is essential to understand that consenting romantic and sexual relationships between employees in senior or supervisory positions and less-senior or lower-level employees may lead to unforeseen complications. The respect and trust accorded a more senior or supervisory person by a less senior employee, as well as the power held by that person in evaluating or otherwise supervising the less senior person could diminish the extent to which the less senior employee really feels free to choose. Therefore, the Company strongly discourages romantic or sexual relationships between its employees or between any of its managers or other supervisory employees and their staff (i.e., an employee who reports directly or indirectly to that person) because such relationships tend to create compromising conflicts of interest or the appearance of such conflicts. For the purposes of this policy, supervisory responsibilities include, but are not limited to, giving work assignments, directing performance, reviewing performance, disciplining, recommending promotions or raises in pay, approving leave, and scheduling hours of work.
If any employee of the Company enters into a consensual romantic or sexual relationship with (i) an employee whom they directly or indirectly supervise, or (ii) an employee within the same department where one of the individuals has supervisory or managerial authority (including within Human Resources), the parties must promptly disclose the relationship to Human Resources. If the relationship involves an employee within Human Resources, the disclosure must instead be made to april's Senior Legal Counsel. An employee who enters into a sexual relationship with another employee or with someone in a supervisory position where there exists a difference in seniority or authority between the individuals involved should be aware that, if a complaint of sexual harassment is subsequently made, it could be exceedingly difficult to prove immunity on grounds of mutual consent.
Once the relationship is disclosed to the Company, the Company will review the situation with Human Resources or the Senior Legal Counsel, as applicable, in light of all the facts (i.e., reporting relationship between the parties, effect on co-workers, job titles of the parties, etc.) and will take appropriate action. Violations of this policy may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.
Additionally, intimate behaviour during work time (such as holding hands, kissing, other close physical contact and discussions of a sexual nature, etc.) is not permitted. 3. Full day disciplinary suspensions for infractions of safety rules of major significance (i.e., those relating to the prevention of serious danger in the workplace or to other employees);
4. Full day disciplinary suspensions imposed in good faith for infractions of workplace conduct rules, where the suspension is imposed pursuant to a written policy applicable to all employees;
5. Family and Medical Leave absences (either full or partial day absences);
6. To offset amounts received as payment for jury and witness fees or military pay; or
7. The first or last week of employment in the event of less than a full week worked.
Exempt salaried employees' salaries are also subject to reduction for their portion of health, dental, or life insurance premiums; state, federal, or local taxes; social security; or voluntary contributions to a 401(k) or pension plan.
In any workweek in which exempt salaried employees perform any work, their salary is not subject to reduction for any of the following reasons:
1. Partial day absences for personal reasons, sickness, or disability;
2. Absence due to april's decision to close a facility on a scheduled workday;
3. Absences for jury duty, attendance as a witness, or military leave in any week in which any work is performed; or
4. Any other deductions prohibited by state or federal law.
However, subject to state law, it is not an improper deduction to reduce an exempt employee's accrued vacation, personal, or other forms of paid time off as a result of the employee's full or partial day absences for personal reasons, sickness, or disability.
Employees who believe they have been subject to an improper deduction should report the matter to their supervisor immediately. If the supervisor is unavailable or is an inappropriate person to contact, or if a prompt and fully acceptable reply has not been received within five (5) business days, Human Resources should be contacted.
Every report of improper deductions will be fully investigated and corrective action, up to and including discharge, will be taken, as appropriate, for any employee(s) who violates this policy. In addition, The Company will not allow any form of retaliation against individuals who report alleged violations of this policy or who cooperate in april's investigation of such reports. # III. Business Conduct

The Company operates in a competitive business environment, and it is important that we protect the confidential nature of proprietary and trade secret information. april's rights to this information are protected by state law. This protection extends to all information or materials that are generally not made available to the public and are of value or use in the business of the Company. As an employee, you are expected to protect the confidentiality of this information and not to improperly disclose such information.

Information of the Company or any Company Affiliates concerning its processes, products or services, operations, accounting, research and development, suppliers, customers or prospects, terms and conditions of sale and prices, purchasing, marketing, brokering and selling of processes, products or services, and knowledge of markets for its products and services.
Employees are expected to hold confidential information in the strictest confidence and may not, either directly or indirectly, disclose, publish, reproduce, transfer or furnish to any unauthorized person or entity any such confidential information or otherwise use any such confidential information except as necessary in the performance of their professional duties for the Company, except to the extent an employee has received express prior written permission from Human Resources to do so. ## 2. Conflict of Interest
Employees have an obligation to conduct business within guidelines that prohibit actual or potential conflicts of interest.
A "conflict of interest" is anything that impairs an employee's judgment with regard to the Company best interest. For example, a conflict of interest occurs when an employee, involved in april's business dealings, intentionally influences a business decision for personal gain. Personal gain means an outcome that would benefit the employee and/or the relatives, friends, or other personal or business contacts of the employee.
An employee is not considered to have a conflict of interest and/or to be in violation of this policy by the mere existence of a relationship with a business contact of the Company. If you determine that you have such a relationship, it is imperative that you immediately disclose the existence of this relationship to Human Resources. At that time, it will be determined whether any actual or potential conflict of interest exists, so that safeguards can be established to protect all parties involved.
Failing to disclose a conflict of interest may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment. Certain employees may be required to sign a formal conflicts of interest statement.
## 3. Gifts and Entertainment
The Company recognizes that, in the ordinary course of business, employees may be offered gifts or entertainment by vendors, customers, or other business partners. While such gestures may be intended as goodwill, employees must exercise good judgment and ensure that accepting or offering gifts or entertainment does not influence, or appear to influence, business judgment or create a conflict of interest.
Employees may accept modest and infrequent gifts or entertainment, provided they are reasonable in value, consistent with customary business practices, and do not create an actual or perceived conflict of interest. Employees may not solicit gifts, favors, or entertainment.
Gifts or entertainment must not be accepted or provided if they:
1. could be construed as a bribe, payoff, or improper inducement;
2. violate applicable law, regulation, or Company policy; or
3. otherwise create a conflict of interest or the appearance of impropriety.
Employees should promptly report any offer or receipt of gifts or entertainment that may raise a concern to Human Resources.
**Business Gifts**
Business gifts may be accepted if they are nominal in value, infrequent, and consistent with this policy. Perishable or shared items (e.g., food or flowers) should be shared where appropriate. Items provided for legitimate business purposes (e.g., product samples or evaluation materials) may be accepted if used for Company business.
Gifts that are excessive, inappropriate, or otherwise inconsistent with this policy should be politely declined or returned. If return is not feasible, the Company may determine an appropriate alternative, including reimbursement to the giver, to the extent permitted by applicable law.
Employees may not accept or offer anything of value that is tied to business decisions, preferential treatment, or an expectation of future business.
**Entertainment**
Business entertainment (e.g., meals or attendance at events) may be accepted if it is reasonable, not excessive, and has a legitimate business purpose, such as fostering business relationships. Generally, the individual or entity providing the entertainment should be present.
**Employee-to-Employee Gifts**
Voluntary gifts exchanged between employees (e.g., for holidays or personal occasions) are not reimbursable and should be made at the employee's discretion and expense.
Employees should ensure that any such gifts are reasonable, voluntary, and not intended to influence, or appear to influence, employment-related decisions (such as performance evaluations, compensation, promotions, or assignments), or otherwise create a conflict of interest or the appearance of impropriety. Employees may not solicit gifts from other employees.

Employees may not engage in outside employment or business activities that compete with the Company, involve the use of Company confidential information, or otherwise conflict with april's business interests, to the fullest extent permitted by applicable law.

The employment of family members/relatives may cause conflicts of interest, personal conflicts, and limit the diversity of the Company's workforce. The Company believes in hiring and promoting people based on their knowledge, skills, abilities, and potential. As such, the Company wishes to reduce the potential conflicts of interest that may occur when family members/relatives work together.
For the purpose of this policy, a "family member" is defined as any familial relation by blood or marriage. Other family or close personal relationships will be considered on a case-by-case basis.
The Company permits hiring of family members/relatives of current employees. However, no family members/relatives shall:
- Work in the same department or share a manager/supervisor;
- Have any reporting relationship between them;
- Oversee processes that will affect a family member/relative; or
  - For example, a Human Resources employee may not be a business partner, employee relations manager, or compensation supervisor over any department that the family member/relative is in.
- Participate in any disciplinary, promotion, or reward decision that directly affects the family member/relative.
When dealing with outside firms, either as vendors, clients, or service providers, these same guidelines shall apply.
If a new relationship violates this policy (i.e., a new romantic relationship, a marriage that creates a new family relationship, etc.), the employee(s) must report the change to The Company as soon as possible. The Company will determine whether any actual or potential conflict of interest exists, so that safeguards can be established to protect all parties involved.
Failing to disclose a conflict of interest may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment. Certain employees may be required to sign a formal conflicts of interest statement.
If you have any concerns regarding this policy, please contact Human Resources.

# IV. Attendance / Time Off
## 1. Punctuality, Attendance and Reporting an Absence or Tardiness
Punctuality and attendance are critical in maintaining a normal workload and efficient schedule and will be considered in evaluating your performance. Employees are expected to report to work regularly and to be ready to perform their assigned duties at the beginning of each workday. This includes arriving at work on time, taking only permitted breaks, and leaving work at the approved hour. Employees are responsible for arriving at work every workday unless their absence is excused or permitted under april's time off policies or is protected by applicable law.
If an employee is going to be absent or late, it is the employee's responsibility to inform their manager in advance. If an employee is unable to send the initial message or email the Company requests that a family member or friend, do it for the employee. If an employee is unable to reach their manager, they should speak to another member of management.
If an employee is absent for several consecutive days, the employee must inform their supervisor each day (unless specifically instructed otherwise such as during an approved leave of absence). Failure to notify the supervisor may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment. If an employee is absent from work for three (3) days without notifying their manager or another member of management, then depending on the circumstances, such employee may be considered to have voluntarily abandoned their employment with the Company.
Excessive and/or unexcused tardiness or absences may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment.
## 2. Severe Weather
At any given time, when one of our locations may be experiencing bad weather, there still are people trying to do business with or otherwise interact with our staff. Thus, we will try to keep all offices open within normal business hours whenever possible. However, we also want to ensure the safety of our employees; therefore, it may be necessary at times to either delay opening or to close early. Notifications surrounding delays or office closings will be made via Slack. If the office is open and you are unable to report to work on time due to inclement weather, you must promptly notify your manager of your anticipated arrival time or the need for a personal or vacation day. Working remotely is subject to your manager's written direction.
## 3. Flexible Time Off ("FTO" or "Vacation")
FTO (i.e., vacation time) is a valuable benefit that provides you time away from work to relax, rejuvenate, and spend time travelling, volunteering, or pursuing other personal interests outside from work. To recognize and meet the varying needs of our employees, we provide FTO as described below.
**Eligibility**
All full-time employees who are regularly scheduled to work at least 30 hours a week are eligible to participate in april's FTO policy. Part-time and temporary employees regularly scheduled to work 20 hours or more per week earn prorated vacation based on their standard weekly hours. Interns are not eligible for vacation.
april offers FTO to eligible employees. Rather than accruing a set number of vacation days, employees are trusted to take the time they need to rest, recharge, and attend to personal matters, provided they meet their job responsibilities and business needs are met. There is no set number of FTO days allocated per year, and employees do not accrue or carry over FTO balances. FTO is not considered additional wages or earned compensation. FTO is not paid out upon termination, resignation, or separation from employment for any reason. FTO has no cash value and cannot be converted to monetary compensation. There is no fixed limit on the amount of FTO available, but employees must meet performance expectations. FTO does not apply to absences related to illness, injury, family medical needs, or leaves of absence, which are governed by separate policies outlined in this document.
**Scheduling FTO**
When you want to take a vacation, you should talk to your manager as far in advance as possible, so that your department can plan to cover your assignments while you are away. All vacation requests must be submitted through JustWorks for manager review and approval, subject to business needs. You must provide at least five (5) business days' notice to your manager if the need to use FTO is foreseeable. Every effort will be made to grant vacation requests but there may be situations when operational needs may result in a denial of a request. Further, we reserve the right to mandate the use of vacation time in certain instances, to the extent permitted by applicable law. All requests for vacation time must be approved by the employee's manager in april's time and attendance system.
All FTO must be recorded in JustWorks for both full and partial days taken. The FTO taken in excess of two consecutive weeks will be considered unpaid vacation or a leave of absence and will be governed by the applicable leave of absence policy.
FTO exceeding two consecutive weeks will be treated as unpaid time off or a leave of absence. Employees planning extended absences should consult with their manager and the People Team well in advance. The Company may limit or deny FTO during critical business periods such as tax season, quarter-end close, product launches, or other high-demand periods.
**No Accrual or Payout**
Because FTO is unlimited and does not accrue, there is no FTO balance to carry over, and unused FTO will not be paid out upon separation from the Company, unless required by applicable law.
## 4. Paid Time Off ("PTO") for Temporary Employees
Temporary employees earn time off through an accrual system based on hours worked. This ensures compliance with applicable wage and hour laws while providing predictable, earned benefits. All temporary employees begin accruing time off with their first hour of work.
Temporary employees earn general-purpose PTO according to the following structure:
- **Accrual Rate:** Employees earn 1 hour of PTO for every 8 hours worked. Accrual begins immediately upon hire. PTO accrues for all hours worked, including overtime hours.
- **Maximum Accrual and Carryover:** There is no maximum accrual cap for PTO. PTO carries over from year to year without limitation. Unused PTO does not expire while employed.
- **Usage:** PTO may be used for any purpose, including vacation, personal business, or other non-medical needs. Employees must request and receive manager approval before using accrued PTO. PTO may not be used before it is accrued.
- **Payout Upon Separation:** Accrued but unused PTO will be paid out upon termination, resignation, or separation from employment. Payout will be calculated at the employee's final base rate of pay. Payout is subject to all applicable taxes and withholdings.
## 5. Holidays
The Company typically observes the following paid holidays each calendar year. Full-time employees are eligible to receive holiday pay when the holiday falls on, or is celebrated on, the employee's regular workday:
1. New Year's Day
2. Memorial Day
3. Juneteenth
4. Independence Day
5. Labor Day
6. Thanksgiving Day
7. Day After Thanksgiving
8. Christmas Eve
9. Christmas Day
If the holiday falls on Saturday it will be celebrated the previous Friday. If the holiday falls on Sunday it will be celebrated the following Monday.
When holidays fall or are celebrated on an eligible employee's regular workday, such employee will receive one day's pay at their regular straight-time rate. Non-exempt employees who are called in to work on a holiday will receive one day's pay at their regular straight-time rate, and an additional payment of straight-time for the actual time they work that day.
Except where an employee actually works on a holiday, holidays do not count as time worked for purposes of determining overtime pay and will only be paid if the holiday falls on a normal business day.
## 6. Safe/Sick Time
The Company provides paid safe/sick time to all employees who work in a calendar year (i.e., January 1 through December 31).
To the extent any applicable paid sick time/leave law or ordinance provides any greater rights than set forth in this policy, such differences are addressed in a separate state supplement policy, and if not, then the Company will advise you of such benefits. In any event, said employees will receive the greater benefit. In all instances, the Company will comply with applicable law.
*New York:* Employees should consult the New York State Supplement for april's policy in New York.
*California:* Employees should consult the California State Supplement for april's policy in California.
**Eligible Absences**
All employees covered under this policy may use paid safe/sick time for absences for the following "covered reasons":
1. The employee's mental or physical illness, injury or health condition or need for medical diagnosis, care or treatment of a mental or physical illness, injury or health condition or need for preventive medical care;
2. The care of the employee's family member who needs medical diagnosis, care or treatment of a mental or physical illness, injury or health condition or who needs preventive medical care;
3. The employee or a family member of the employee being the victim of family offense matters, sexual offenses, stalking, or human trafficking:
  1. To obtain services from a domestic violence shelter, rape crisis center, or other shelter or services program for relief from a family offense matter, sexual offense, stalking, or human trafficking;
  2. To participate in safety planning, temporarily relocate, or take other actions to increase the safety of the employee or employee's family members from future family offense matters, sexual offenses, stalking, or human trafficking;
  3. To meet with a civil attorney or other social service provider to obtain information and advice on, and prepare for or participate in any criminal or civil proceeding, including but not limited to, matters related to a family offense matter, sexual offense, stalking, human trafficking, custody, visitation, matrimonial issues, orders of protection, immigration, housing, discrimination in employment, housing or consumer credit;
  4. To file a complaint or domestic incident report with law enforcement;
  5. To meet with a district attorney's office;
  6. To enroll children in a new school; or
  7. To take other actions necessary to maintain, improve, or restore the physical, psychological, or economic health or safety of the employee or employee's family member or to protect those who associate or work with the employee.
4. Closure of the employee's place of business by order of a public official due to a public health emergency or such employee's need to care for a child whose school or childcare provider has been closed by order of a public official due to a public health emergency.
5. Any other covered reason allowed under applicable law.
For purposes of this policy, "family member" means a child, spouse, domestic partner, parent, sibling (including half siblings, step siblings, or siblings related through adoption), grandchild, grandparent, or the child or parent of the employee's spouse or domestic partner, any other individual related by blood to the employee, and any other individual whose close association with the employee is the equivalent of a family relationship.
Employees are not permitted to use safe/sick time as additional vacation and/or personal days. Safe/sick time may only be used for the reasons set forth above.
**Annual Allotment**
Full-time, part-time, and temporary employees receive five (5) days of safe/sick days at the time of hire and at the beginning of each calendar year thereafter.
**Carryover**
Unused safe/sick time under this policy will be carried over to the following calendar year.
**Utilization**
Unless applicable law requires otherwise:
- For all employees covered under this policy, paid safe/sick time may be used in a minimum increment of four (4) hours, provided this is reasonable under the circumstances.
- For uses beyond four (4) hours, paid safe/sick time may be used in thirty (30) minutes increments (i.e., 4.5 hours, 5 hours, 5.5 hours etc.).
Absences taken under this policy are not subject to the Punctuality, Attendance and Reporting an Absence or Tardiness Policy.
Employees may not borrow safe/sick time beyond the safe/sick time allotment the Company provides upon hire and at the beginning of every calendar year. Employees who have exhausted their safe/sick time for the year will have the option of taking a vacation day, personal day or an unpaid day. Employees who subsequently accrue safe/sick days will not be permitted to exchange a safe/sick day for a previously used vacation or personal day, which the employee opted to utilize in lieu of a safe/sick day.
**Notice and Documentation**
Employees should notify their manager of their need for leave as early as practicable.
The Company will not require that employees, or a healthcare or service provider, disclose personal health information or the details of the matter for which an employee requests leave under the law. The Company must keep information about an employee or an employee's family member obtained solely because of use of safe and sick leave confidential unless the employee consents to disclosure in writing or disclosure is required by law.
For paid safe/sick time used for reasons (1) or (2) above, documentation signed by a licensed health care provider indicating the need for the amount of paid safe/sick time taken and that paid safe/sick time was used for an authorized purpose under this policy and/or applicable law will be considered reasonable documentation, and such documentation need not specify the nature of the employee's or the employee's family member's injury, illness or condition, except as required by law. For safe/sick used time for reason (3) above, documentation signed by an employee, agent, or volunteer of a victim services organization, an attorney, a member of the clergy, or a medical or other professional service provider from whom the employee or that employee's family member has sought assistance in addressing family offense matters, sex offenses, stalking, or human trafficking and their effects; a police or court record; or a notarized letter from the employee explaining the need for such time will be considered reasonable documentation, and such documentation need not specify the details of the family offense matter, sexual offense, stalking, or human trafficking. Regarding reason (4), the Company will advise employees regarding what documentation to submit.
For any employee covered under this policy, failure to provide requested documentation for paid safe/sick time taken under this policy within (7) seven days of returning to work may result in disciplinary action, up to and including termination.
Additionally, the Company may require any employee covered under this policy to provide written confirmation that an employee used paid safe/sick time in accordance with applicable law.
An employee's use of safe/sick time will not be conditioned upon searching for or finding a replacement worker.
The Company may take disciplinary action, up to and including termination, against an employee who uses safe/sick time provided under this policy for purposes other than those described above. Indications of abuse of safe/sick time may include, but are not limited to, a pattern of: (1) use of unscheduled safe/sick time on or adjacent to weekends, regularly scheduled days off, holidays, vacation or pay day, (2) taking scheduled safe/sick time on days when other leave has been denied, or (3) taking safe/sick time on days when the employee is scheduled to work a shift or perform duties perceived as undesirable.
**Tracking and Recording Safe/Sick Days**
All employees are responsible for tracking their utilization of safe/sick leave. All managers must track and record all of their employees' absences in accordance with april's time-recording practice.
**Payment**
Paid safe/sick time will be paid at the same rate as the employee earns from the employee's employment at the time the employee uses such time, but no less than the applicable minimum wage.
Use of paid safe/sick time is not considered hours worked for purposes of calculating overtime.
Unused paid safe/sick time under this policy will not be paid upon separation of employment for any reason. The Company does not offer pay in lieu of using leave under this policy.
**No Retaliation**
The Company prohibits retaliation or the threat of retaliation against an employee for exercising or attempting to exercise any right provided in this policy, or interference with any investigation, proceeding or hearing related to or arising out of employee's rights pursuant to this policy and applicable law.
Employees with questions concerning this policy should contact Human Resources.
**Sick Leave for Temporary Employees**
Temporary employees earn sick leave according to the following structure:
- **Accrual Rate:** Employees earn 1 hour of sick leave for every 20 hours worked. Accrual begins immediately upon hire. Sick leave accrues for all hours worked, including overtime hours.
- **Maximum Accrual and Carryover:** Maximum accrual is capped at 5 days (40 hours for full-time equivalent). Once the cap is reached, no additional sick leave will accrue until the balance falls below the cap. Maximum carryover from year to year is 5 days (40 hours). Accrual years are measured based on the calendar year or employee anniversary date, as determined by applicable law.
- **Usage:** Sick leave may be used for personal illness, injury, medical appointments, or care for family members as permitted by applicable law. Employees should notify their manager as soon as practicable when sick leave is needed. Medical documentation may be required for extended absences, consistent with applicable law.
- **Payout Upon Separation:** Accrued but unused sick leave will not be paid out upon termination or separation.

Eligibility requirements vary by type of leave of absence. Employees will continue to accrue vacation, sick days during an approved period of unpaid or partially paid leave.
The Company has the right to investigate all leaves. Misrepresentation and/or falsification of a leave can result in disciplinary actions, up to and including cancellation of the leave and termination of employment.

## 8. Short Term Disability Leave
The Company will comply with all applicable federal, state, and local laws regarding short-term disability benefits. To the extent required by law, eligible employees will be provided short-term disability benefits in accordance with those legal requirements.
Short-term disability benefits provide wage replacement only and do not guarantee job protection. Leave taken in connection with a disability may run concurrently with leave under any applicable federal, state, or local leave laws, to the extent permitted by law.
Employees may be required to provide medical certification supporting the need for disability leave and may be required to provide periodic updates and/or a fitness-for-duty certification before returning to work, consistent with applicable law. Disabilities arising out of or in the course of employment are generally addressed under workers' compensation and are not covered by short-term disability benefits.
Please direct all questions concerning this policy to Human Resources.
## 9. Worker's Compensation Leave
Workers' Compensation provides all employees for payment of medical expenses and in some cases partial payment for time lost due to illness, accident, or injury incurred in the course of employment. All employees are covered under our Workers' Compensation policy, which is paid for by the Company. Workers' Compensation is governed by various state laws. At no point may any combination of benefits provide an employee more than 100% of their weekly salary.
Any injury, accident, or illness occurring at the worksite or while traveling on business must be reported immediately to your Manager. A written report containing complete details of the accident and injury or illness must be sent to Human Resources within 24 hours of the incident. Failure to report any accident, illness, or injury may affect an employee's eligibility for any Workers' Compensation benefits to which they may be entitled.
An employee on a Workers' Compensation leave will be put in touch with our Workers' Compensation insurance carrier who will require that you obtain a physician's statement showing the date leave will begin and end. The physician's statement should be immediately submitted to Human Resources.
Reinstatement following Workers' Compensation Leave is consistent with any applicable federal or state leave law running concurrently. Upon the employee's return, the employee must present a physician's statement to Human Resources indicating that they have been released to return to work. Human Resources will review the release and advise the manager of any restrictions or limitations.
The Company works with its Workers' Compensation insurer to investigate any suspected fraudulent workers' compensation claims. Employees who file fraudulent claims will be subject to disciplinary action, up to and including an unpaid suspension or termination of employment.
The Company or its insurance carrier will not be liable for the payment of Workers' Compensation benefits for any injury that arises out of your voluntary participation in any off-duty recreational, social or athletic activity that is not part of your work duties.
## 10. Military Leave
An unpaid military leave of absence will be granted to employees who are absent from work because of service in the U.S. uniformed services in accordance with the Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act ("USERRA") and any applicable state or local laws. USERRA protects civilian job rights and benefits for veterans and members of reserve components. Service members who return to their employment within the prescribed period of time are entitled to be reemployed in the job they would have attained had they not been absent for military service, with the same seniority, status and pay, as well as other rights and benefits determined by seniority. Advance notice of military service is required, unless military necessity prevents such notice or it is otherwise impossible or unreasonable. Such notice should be provided to Human Resources, the employee's supervisor and Human Resources. Employees may use any available FTO for the absence.
The Company will maintain the employee's benefits under the same conditions as if they were actively working, subject to the terms, conditions and limitations of the applicable plans for which the employee is otherwise eligible. Employees will be responsible for paying their portion of any insurance premiums while on unpaid leave.
Benefit accruals, such as sick leave, or holiday benefits, will be suspended during the leave and will resume upon the employee's return to active employment.
Employees on military leave for up to thirty (30) days are required to return to work for the first regularly scheduled day's work after the end of service, allowing reasonable travel time. Employees on longer military leave must apply for reinstatement in accordance with USERRA and all applicable state laws.
Employees returning from military leave will be placed in the position they would have attained had they remained continuously employed or a comparable one depending on the length of military service in accordance with USERRA. They will be treated as though they were continuously employed for purposes of determining benefits based on length of service. If an employee accepts other employment during any period of military leave, they will be deemed to have voluntarily resigned from the Company.
USERRA is detailed, comprehensive, and complex. Contact Human Resources for more information or questions about military leave.
## 11. Jury Duty Leave
The Company recognizes jury service as an important civic responsibility and will comply with all federal, state, and local requirements governing jury duty leave and related protections. Employees who receive a jury summons must promptly notify their supervisor and Human Resources and provide a copy of the summons so that appropriate coverage arrangements can be made. Employees are expected to keep the Company informed regarding the anticipated length of service and must provide reasonable verification of attendance.
Employees will receive pay for time spent on jury duty in accordance with applicable law. On any day or portion of a day during which an employee is not required to report for jury service, or is excused early, the employee may be required to report to work if reasonably able to do so. Upon completion of jury service, employees are expected to return to work on their next scheduled workday, unless a different return schedule is mandated.
Jury duty leave does not constitute hours worked for purposes of calculating overtime unless required under governing wage and hour rules. Exempt employees will receive their full salary for any workweek in which they perform work, consistent with salary basis requirements.
The Company will not discharge, discipline, threaten, coerce, or otherwise discriminate or retaliate against any employee because of jury service or for exercising rights protected by law. Employees who believe their rights have been violated should report the concern to Human Resources.

The Company will provide bereavement leave in accordance with applicable law.

## 14. Voting Leave
The Company believes that it is the responsibility and duty of employees to exercise the privilege of voting in elections. In accordance with this philosophy, the Company will grant its employees approved time off to vote if necessary due to work schedules and for periods of service as an election official in compliance with applicable law.
Unless applicable law requires greater benefits, all employees are eligible for up to two (2) hours of unpaid time off to vote if they do not have "sufficient time to vote." An employee is deemed to have "sufficient time to vote" if an employee has four (4) consecutive hours to vote either from the opening of the polls to the beginning of their work shift, or four (4) consecutive hours between the end of a working shift and the closing of the polls.
An employee must notify an employer at least two (2) working days prior to their intention to take FTO to vote, but not more than ten (10) working days.
For questions, employees should contact Human Resources.
